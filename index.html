Questions that drive me

Selected research

Connecting mathematical models with biological and clinical questions.

01 /

Probabilistic immune-cell trajectories

My master’s thesis uses patient-specific TCR clonotypes as anchors for single-cell trajectory inference. I implement rooted shortest paths, minimum spanning trees, and KMB Steiner trees on a Harmony-space kNN graph of more than 155,000 T cells, and am developing an MCMC framework to quantify uncertainty across alternative immune-state connections.

The work analyzes longitudinal Long COVID PBMC data from 16 patients and combines scRNA-seq, TCR metadata, and RNA/ADT annotation review.

02 /

Single-cell representation and perturbation modeling

I study how transcriptomic representations capture interpretable biological structure. My work includes Perturb-seq quality control, PCA and feature-loading analysis, and comparisons of pretrained scGPT embeddings with sparse dictionary learning and processed gene-expression features.

I build reproducible Python and R workflows for analysis, model evaluation, and interpretation.

Selected projects

TriAlign: Clinical trial eligibility representation learning

Developing retrieval and comparison workflows that distinguish clinical meaning from surface-level textual similarity across trial protocols. The project combines dense embeddings, structured rules, and LLM judgments to identify equivalence, restrictiveness, overlap, contradiction, and ambiguity.

Evaluation emphasizes hard negatives, leakage-resistant trial splits, human-reviewed labels, calibration, and abstention.

Python · PyTorch · FAISS · Pydantic

Type 2 diabetes risk modeling

Modeling 12-month T2D risk using self-collectable demographic, anthropometric, and survey features from the NIH All of Us Research Program. I use ridge-penalized logistic regression and restricted cubic splines, selecting screening thresholds under prespecified sensitivity constraints.

Evaluation includes discrimination, calibration, Brier score, screening burden, and comparisons with ADA and USPSTF criteria.

scGPT vs. sparse dictionary learning

Comparing pretrained scGPT embeddings, sparse dictionary learning, and processed gene-expression features on public single-cell RNA-seq data. Matched representations are evaluated with regularized multinomial logistic regression, Macro-F1, balanced accuracy, and log loss.

I examine sparse dictionary atoms, high-weight genes, and cell-type activation patterns to interpret gene programs.

Python · PyTorch · AnnData · scGPT
